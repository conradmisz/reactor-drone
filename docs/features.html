Drone

One maintenance drone. Twenty waves. Four arenas that each want you dead in a different colour.

You fly a small drone inside a failing reactor. Enemies pour in from the rim in waves, you shoot them, they drop credits, and every few waves the run pauses and lets you spend those credits on something that makes you harder to kill. Clear twenty waves and you win. Lose your hull and you don't.

Section 01

Quick start

Enough to start playing right now. Everything after this is detail.

In ten seconds

  1. Pick Normal or Hard on the title screen — or just press Space for Normal.
  2. Move with WASD or the arrow keys.
  3. Aim with the mouse. The drone always faces your cursor.
  4. Hold left mouse to fire. You can hold it down the whole run.
  5. Walk over the credits enemies drop. Spend them when the run pauses.

Two things kill new players: standing still, and forgetting that touching an enemy hurts. Keep moving, and treat every enemy as a thing you must not bump into.

Section 03

A run, start to finish

Every run is the same shape. Learning where the pauses fall is most of learning the game.

  1. Choose a difficulty. Normal or Hard, once, at the title. The run keeps that setting even if you restart from a death.
  2. Fight a wave. Enemies spawn out at the rim of the arena and come for you. Early waves send a set number of them; later ones just keep coming for a fixed stretch of time.
  3. Clear the arena. A wave isn't over when the last one spawns — it's over when the last one dies. Stragglers hiding at the far rim are still your problem. If something gets truly stuck, the reactor eventually scraps it for you.
  4. Take the pause. Every fourth cleared wave the run stops and asks: open the shop, or push straight on? Nothing moves until you choose.
  5. Spend, or don't. Banking credits for something expensive is a real strategy. So is buying a cheap hull patch right now because you're at a sliver of health.
  6. Change arenas. Every five waves the whole arena crossfades into a new one — new colours, new obstacle layout, new hazards, and enemies re-tinted to match.
  7. Reach wave twenty. Clear it and the run is a win. Lose your hull anywhere along the way and it's over — click or press Space to start again.

Section 04

Staying alive

Hull

Your health. It does not regenerate on its own — the only ways back up are a Hull Plating purchase (which repairs as well as raises your maximum) and a Repair Kit. Treat every point as spent for good until you reach a shop.

Shields

Bought, not given. Once you own a Shield Capacitor you get a pool that soaks damage before your hull does, and it refills on its own — but only after you've gone a few seconds without being hit. Shields reward disengaging. Take a hit, break away, come back topped up.

Being saved by your shield still counts as being hit: you still flash, the screen still shakes, and the regen delay still resets. A shield is a buffer, not invulnerability.

Contact damage

Enemies hurt you by touching you — there are no enemy projectiles. Every archetype does a different amount on contact, and the big slow ones hurt most.

Invulnerability window

After a hit you get a brief window where you can't be hurt again. It's short, and it's the reason brushing through a crowd is survivable while sitting inside one is not.

Hazards don't move, and they don't care. Every arena has fixed hazard patches — vents, pools, glowing floor. They damage you on contact exactly like an enemy does, they're always there, and on Hard they hurt considerably more. Learn where they are in each arena; a panicked reverse into one is a very common death.

Obstacles and the wall

The pillars and blocks scattered through each arena are solid. You can't fly through them and neither can your shots — but neither can enemies, and they have to walk around. Used well, an obstacle is cover. Used badly, it's the thing you back into while a swarm closes. The arena's outer wall is a hard boundary: you cannot leave, and there is no corner to hide in.

Section 05

What's shooting at you

Nothing, technically — everything in the reactor kills by running into you. There are three archetypes, and they trade speed against toughness in the obvious way.

Fast · fragile Spark

The quickest thing in the arena and the flimsiest. Dies to a couple of shots, but closes distance faster than you can back away in a straight line. Worth the least.

Middle Runner

The default. Tougher than a Spark, slower than one, and worth more when it dies. Most of a wave is usually these.

Slow · tanky Hulk

Big, ponderous and by far the hardest to kill. Hurts most on contact and pays out most on death — it's the one enemy that reliably drops loot. Kite it; don't trade with it.

They all seek you directly when they can see you, and route around obstacles when they can't. You can't lose them behind a pillar — you can only slow them down and make them arrive strung out instead of all at once, which is usually the whole fight.

Enemies wear the arena's colour. Their shape tells you what they are; the palette tells you where you are. In the fourth arena they cycle through the whole spectrum continuously, which is exactly as legible as it sounds and exactly as intended.

Section 06

Waves & difficulty

Twenty waves, two kinds

The first stretch of the run sends a fixed number of enemies: kill them all and the wave is done. The back half switches to timed waves — enemies keep arriving for a set stretch of time, and the wave only ends once the stream stops and you've cleared what's left. Timed waves are where runs usually end, because you can't rush them; you can only survive them.

Difficulty climbs continuously across the twenty: more enemies, arriving closer together, with more health and more speed, and the tougher archetypes start appearing in the mix earlier than they did. By the last few waves you're facing the whole roster at once, considerably tougher than it started.

Section 07

Credits & loot

Dead enemies scatter credit pickups on the floor. You collect them by flying over them — nothing is collected automatically, and nothing is collected from across the arena. That single design choice is what makes the economy interesting: the credits are always lying exactly where the fighting just was, and going to get them means going back in.

Not every kill drops something. Tougher enemies drop more reliably and are worth more when they do. Pickups sit on the floor for a while and then expire, so loot you ignore during a hectic wave is genuinely gone.

Shop keys. Rarely — and it is rare — an enemy drops a key instead. A key lets you open the shop whenever you like with B, without waiting for the next scheduled pause. Spending one mid-wave freezes the fight where it stands, so a key is an emergency repair as much as it is a shopping trip.

Section 08

The shop

The shop opens at the between-waves pause, every fourth cleared wave — or any time you spend a key. While it's open the arena is completely frozen: nothing spawns, nothing moves, nothing can hurt you. Take as long as you want.

  1. Press Tab to switch pages. Page one is upgrades, page two is gear.
  2. Press the row number to buy. Keys 1 through 8 map to the rows on the page you're looking at.
  3. Press B to leave. That launches the next wave. (Space deliberately does nothing here — you're probably still holding it.)

How pricing works

Upgrades are permanent and stackable, and each copy of a given upgrade costs meaningfully more than the last. Buying your fifth Hull Plating is an expensive decision in a way the first one wasn't. Each upgrade also has a cap — you cannot stack any single one forever.

Gear works differently: items and consumables are equipped, not stacked, so they have a flat price and no escalation. You hold one item and one consumable at a time, and buying a new one replaces what you were carrying.

Prices are still being tuned. The whole catalogue is provisional while the balance settles, so treat the relative costs here as the shape of the thing rather than fixed numbers.

Section 09

Upgrades

Six permanent upgrades, page one of the shop. All stackable up to a cap, all more expensive each time.

SurvivabilityHull Plating

Raises maximum hull and repairs you on the spot. The only in-shop healing that isn't a consumable, which makes it the reflex buy when you arrive hurt.

SurvivabilityShield Capacitor

Grants (and deepens) the regenerating shield pool. The first one changes how the game plays more than anything else in the shop.

MobilityAux Thruster

Permanently faster. Speed is defence in a game where everything kills by touching you, and it makes collecting loot mid-wave far safer.

DamageOverclock

Fire faster. Scales with everything else you own and never stops being useful.

DamageHeavy Rounds

Each shot hits harder. Better than fire rate against Hulks, worse against a swarm of Sparks.

DamageTwin Barrel

An extra projectile per shot. Easily the most expensive row and capped low — a late-run investment, not an opener.

Section 10

Gear

Page two. You carry one item and one consumable. Buying another of either replaces what you're holding, so gear is a choice rather than a collection.

Items — passive, permanent, always on

Magnet Core

Credits within a generous radius fly toward you instead of waiting to be walked over. Turns the whole loot problem down a notch.

Repulsor Field

Constantly pushes nearby enemies away from you. A gentle shove, not a wall — it buys you room to turn, it does not make you untouchable.

Reactive Plating

Anything that rams you takes damage for it. Works even when your shield ate the hit, so it keeps paying out on a well-shielded build.

Salvager

Every credit pickup is worth more. Compounds over a whole run, which makes it strongest bought early and weakest bought late.

Consumables — one use, spent with Q

EmergencyRepair Kit

Restores a big chunk of hull instantly. The reason to keep a consumable slot filled even when nothing else tempts you.

OffenceOverdrive

A burst of dramatically increased fire rate for a few seconds. Best saved for the moment a timed wave stops being survivable.

OffenceEMP Burst

Damages every living enemy in the arena at once. On an early wave it's a wipe; on a late one it's a dent that buys you space.

EscapePhase Shift

A few seconds of complete invulnerability. Fly straight through the crowd you were trapped in.

Don't hoard it. A consumable you die holding did nothing. There's no penalty for using one early and buying another at the next stop.

Section 11

The four arenas

The run moves through four arenas, one every five waves, crossfading from one into the next mid-run. Each has its own palette, its own obstacle layout and its own hazard placement — and enemies are re-tinted to whichever one is live, always in a colour that contrasts with the floor.

Use the palette buttons in the sidebar to recolour this page to any of them.

Waves 1–5Core

Cyan and magenta, the cleanest layout of the four. Where you learn the game — open sightlines and forgiving hazard placement.

Waves 6–10Foundry

Orange and amber, and the most cluttered floor: more obstacles to route around and more hazards between you and your loot.

Waves 11–15Bio-lab

Green and violet, fewer obstacles but the heaviest hazard coverage in the game. Space to fly, less of it safe.

Waves 16–20Prism

A near-white void where the enemies cycle continuously through every colour. Deliberately disorienting, and the arena you finish in.
